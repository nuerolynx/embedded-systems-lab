# OpenDual Reader mechanical fit study — A0

This is an original enclosure proposal, not a reconstruction of reference hardware. No product photograph, internal measurement, material sample, or physical prototype was supplied. Dimensions below are millimetres. The editable source is [OpenDual-Reader-enclosure.scad](OpenDual-Reader-enclosure.scad); [dimensioned-layout.svg](dimensioned-layout.svg) is a drawing generated directly from the dimension table. OpenSCAD 2021.01 successfully compiled the native [front-cover STL](front-cover.stl) and [backplate STL](backplate.stl) on 2026-09-26, and the [native exploded preview](enclosure-exploded.png) was rendered and visually reviewed. These exports establish that the proposed part geometry compiles; they do not establish complete assembly clearance, tolerance compliance or physical fit. The dimension drawing and analytic checks remain separate from native solid rendering.

All enclosure coordinates start at its upper-left rear corner: x runs right, y down the reader, z toward the front. The PCB origin in KiCad is (100,50). Conversion is enclosure x = KiCad x − 100 + 2.35; enclosure y = KiCad y − 50 + 3.60. The PCB outline, in KiCad millimetres, is (100,50), (136,50), (136,174), (124,174), (124,164), (112,164), (112,174), (100,174), then closed.

The electrical design now uses **six copper layers at the same nominal 1.6 mm PCB thickness**. Layer count does not change the modelled outline, mounting holes or component seating planes. The finished board thickness and its tolerance, including any relevant surface coatings, still require fabrication agreement. With the PCB rear datum held at z4.0, any increase in actual thickness reduces front component headroom by the same amount. The layer change does not resolve the fit blockers below.

Four Ø2.2 non-plated PCB holes are at KiCad (103,99), (133,99), (103,169), (133,169). Reserve Ø6.0 on both PCB faces. They align with Ø5.4 rear bosses and front clamping pillars. Shared countersunk M2 × 16 fasteners are a nominal geometric proposal; screw-head drawing, material, thread form, pilot size and engagement must be verified before fabrication. Prototype nylon hardware is preferred near the antennas, but has not been mechanically qualified. The 1.7 mm printed pilot in the CAD is a trial value, not a released thread dimension. A 0.65 mm locating lip has 0.20 mm side clearance to the cover wall; print and assembly trials are required.

The native PCB's **0.25 mm hole-to-copper minimum** is measured from the Ø2.2 drill edge on every copper layer: a **Ø2.7 no-copper region**, before allowing for trace width. The **Ø6.0 assembly reserve** instead covers components and the Ø5.4 plastic clamping faces on both board surfaces; it does not prohibit inner-layer copper throughout that diameter. Temporary exporter circles of radius 2.4 mm are not an established all-layer mechanical requirement. Keep components, exposed pads and via annuli outside the boss/pillar contact faces. Nylon hardware still applies pressure and can cause wear; solder mask does not guarantee that underlying surface copper is protected. Masked traces beneath a clamp require assembly review. Inner-layer routing outside the drilled-hole clearance follows the normal electrical and fabrication rules. Metal fasteners or washers require renewed clearance and RF review.

**Depth remains an unresolved tolerance check.** The [TRACO TSR 1 datasheet](https://www.tracopower.com/tsr1-datasheet), revision 7 February 2024, page 4, gives a 10.1 mm body height with ±0.5 mm dimensional tolerance. The 10.6 mm maximum originally consumed the entire front clearance: z5.6 + 10.6 = z16.2. Two **14.2 × 10.1 × 0.4 mm internal roof pockets have been applied** above the conservative regulator envelopes, adding 1 mm XY margin per side and leaving 1.0 mm local front thickness. This restores only 0.4 mm headroom. PCB thickness tolerance, solder standoff, enclosure warp and printed-part tolerance remain additional, and the thin areas need strength, printability and thermal checks. An alternative is a lower-height regulator. Lowering the PCB by 0.6 mm would reduce the rear gap to 1.4 mm and worsen lead/harness clearance. The external 17.6 mm depth is unchanged. Do not manufacture this fit study as a closed assembly until the tolerance stack is resolved.

The rear buzzer has a local 10 × 10 × 0.5 mm inner-wall recess, leaving 1.5 mm rear wall and 0.6 mm nominal body clearance. Its left body edge has 0.9 mm nominal clearance to the locating lip after the 0.7 mm placement shift. Package tolerance, solder thickness, lip print tolerance and cable interference require checking. The rear pocket has no acoustic opening; a wall-mounted reader may attenuate it substantially. Do not assume acceptable sound level from the bare buzzer rating.

The final PCB placement moves tall SMBJ suppressors D2/D4/D5 to the front at centres (110,97), (118,97), (126,97), and the SMA diode D1 to front (116.5,161.5). C1 is on the front at (126.5,161.5). This avoids assuming their packages fit the 2 mm rear space. The left and right suppressor centres are 7.28 mm from the nearby M2 pillar centres. Their conservative KiCad courtyard boxes leave approximately 0.625 mm to the Ø5.4 pillar bodies; verify maximum actual lead/body and plastic dimensions. The row puts conductive parts immediately below the HF antenna: its top courtyard edge is y94.725, only 1.725 mm from the antenna's y93 edge. This change must be included in RF tuning; nominal body separation between the two antennas does not describe this added metal. These small packages are not included in the enclosure's coarse body-proxy preview; inspect the populated KiCad 3D board for the complete population. The panel solder pads J1 now start at (101.5,161.5), program pads J2 at (102,80), and the HF harness pads J3 are vertical at x133 beginning y131. Cable fanout to the shifted pads remains a physical assembly check.

**The TVS row is too remote from the cable entry to treat protection as validated.** D2/D4/D5 at y97 are **64.5 mm away in the board's longitudinal direction** from J1 at y161.5; the routed current path is not established by that straight-line difference. Package fit drove the row above the LF module, leaving a long route between the panel cable and its suppressors. Trace and return-path inductance can increase the voltage reaching circuitry during a fast transient. A clean DRC report checks geometry and connectivity, not this transient behaviour. Before fabrication, review a relocation of the suppressors and their short return paths to the J1/notch region, or a separate protection assembly at the cable entry. A small supported assembly near the notch is a possible packaging approach, but its component area, strain relief, insulation, surge return and cable bends have not been allocated or modelled; it cannot simply occupy the jacket's existing volume. No change to the final routed board is implied by this proposal. Validate the complete harness and clamp performance after the protection placement is resolved.

**LF pins need attention.** The ID-3/12/20LA-HE X1.2 dimension table on page 9 gives ID-12LA-HE total depth J = 9.9 nominal / 10.5 maximum and body H = 6.0 nominal / 5.8 minimum / 6.6 maximum. Nominal pin projection J−H is 3.9; the conservative independent-tolerance bound is **10.5−5.8 = 4.7 mm**. Seated at z5.6, the tips can therefore reach z0.9, interfering with the rear plate's inner surface at z2.0 by **1.1 mm** (nominal interference is 0.3 mm). Do not subtract the maximum body height when estimating maximum pin projection. A proposed post-solder trim to 1.0 mm below the PCB gives tip z3.0 and 1.0 mm clearance to the rear plate. Confirm vendor handling allowance and inspect joint fillets; do not trim into the joint or treat this arithmetic as physical verification. All other through-hole lead protrusions require the same check.

**Cable routing drove the bottom notch.** A rectangular board leaves only 2.0 mm between PCB and rear plate, insufficient for a 5 mm jacket to turn into the housing. The accepted 12 × 10 mm bottom notch aligns with the rear oval. The jacket ends in this open volume, secured by a cable tie to the modelled rear bridge; individual insulated conductors fan out to adjacent board pads. The 5 mm oval is a target for a cable no larger than roughly 4 mm after installation clearance, not a certified gland. Determine actual cable diameter, conductor count, conductor insulation, minimum bend radius, connector volume, cable-tie access and jacket strip length on a mock-up. No waterproof gland or ingress rating is claimed. The tie bridge must be proof-loaded and its print orientation reviewed. Do not route wires across RF areas.

**Mounting hardware is a fit and RF dependency.** A head projecting 1.5 mm above the rear plate would leave 0.5 mm to the bare PCB underside, but this does not clear rear-mounted components. The lower wall slot at enclosure (20.35,112), equivalent to PCB (118,158.4), lies beneath the D3 region and near Q2 in the final placement. A normal projecting screw head may interfere with these packages. Its slot, head and component geometry require relocation or a verified flush mounting arrangement before assembly. Slots currently have no head pocket. The upper wall screw is under the ESP32 module region, and metal mullions surround the RF field. Include the complete hardware in physical fit and RF testing.

Rear components have a nominal 2.0 mm space before allowance for solder, pins and enclosure variation. F1's 1.55 mm maximum body alone leaves 0.45 mm; the SOIC transceiver and other rear packages need their own maximum-height stack checks. The coarse enclosure model includes the buzzer and large front modules, not every rear component. A populated KiCad render is a visual aid and does not establish clearance to the backplate or fastener heads.

## RF reservations and material

LF and HF antennas occupy separate planar areas. The nearest nominal body-edge separation is **10.05 mm**: HF ends at KiCad y93 and maximum LF body starts at y103.05. This is a geometric result, not evidence of acceptable coupling. The upper M2 hole centres are only approximately **4.30 mm** from the nearest LF body corners, before accounting for the antenna field. Treat all fasteners, cable shields and metal mounting substrates as RF variables. The illustrated 3 mm halos are provisional placement reserves and may overlap mounting regions; they are not validated electromagnetic boundaries.

Provide separate ferrite evaluation for 125 kHz and 13.56 MHz; do not assume one sheet grade suits both. No ferrite thickness or material is selected here. Reserve the underside volume for trials only after verifying pin and mounting-head clearance. The ESP32 antenna requirement remains independent of both credential antennas; the 36 mm PCB width limits lateral clearance and needs exact-module review. Use an unfilled nonconductive prototype plastic; metallic paint, carbon-filled filament and uncharacterized conductive coatings must be treated as RF changes. Outdoor material, flame, UV, gasket and sealing requirements have not been specified or tested.

## Assembly sequence and checks

1. Print a dimensional coupon for the cover lip, bosses and actual fastener before committing to the two housing pieces. Print rear plate flat with its outside down; inspect the cable-tie bridge and support removal. Choose front orientation and supports after inspecting the long pillars. The CAD is a prototype solid concept, not injection-mould tooling.
2. Establish the final wall screw head clearance. Attach the backplate first while the PCB is removed, feed the unpowered cable, and add the strain-relief tie without crushing the insulation.
3. Solder and inspect modules, then trim approved through-hole leads to the stated envelope. Check pins electrically for shorts and mechanically for sharp burrs.
4. Lay the PCB on its bosses, route individual cable conductors only through the notch, and ensure the 100 mm HF antenna lead has a restrained service loop outside other antenna reserves. Antenna lead shortening is not authorized by this design.
5. Check diffuser seating and tamper actuator travel, then place the front cover and tighten shared fasteners evenly to a tested torque. The selected gold-contact Y31B13136FPLFG / KSC223G LFG SPST-NO switch must be held pressed only when assembled; a remote 1 kΩ series resistor limits capacitor discharge current. With the unpowered loop disconnected from J4, verify approximately 1 kΩ while pressed and an open circuit when released. Do not rely on a continuity beeper that may reject 1 kΩ. No tightening torque, supported switch carrier, preload, hard-stop position or safe overtravel allowance has been established.
6. Measure regulator clearance and case temperature under worst-case load before a closed-housing run. Verify tamper states with the front removed and installed. Run RF tuning with the actual cover, backplate, cable, mounting screws and mullion. Revalidate after any mechanical or ferrite change.

**The remote tamper assembly has no demonstrated fit.** The selected Y31B13136FPLFG / KSC223G LFG body is nominally 6.2 × 6.2 mm and 3.5 mm high. The body alone exceeds the nominal 2.0 mm rear gap by 1.5 mm, before its carrier, solder, retention, clearance and actuator motion. No switch pocket or carrier is modelled in the enclosure CAD. A supported location and actuator arrangement require further mechanical design within the unchanged external envelope; do not assume the switch fits behind the PCB or in the 0.55 mm side clearance.

**The smallest proposed change is a supported front carrier beside U3, retaining the selected switch and the external dimensions.** The candidate strip is KiCad x100.8…107.8, y60…73: a **7 × 13 mm** carrier with the switch terminal axis along y. The manufacturer's G gullwing recommended lands need a 12 mm minimum terminal-axis span, so the earlier 7 × 9 mm suggestion based on the 6.2 mm body is insufficient. The 13 mm length gives only 0.5 mm at each end of that minimum span; the complete maximum package, recommended lands, RT1, wire pads and retention must be laid out before accepting this carrier size. [C&K KSC2 package/land-pattern drawing](https://www.ckswitches.com/media/1968/ksc2.pdf).

A read-only check against the populated PCB found no front or through-hole component pads in that strip. Its right edge is 0.45 mm outside U3's actual courtyard boundary at x108.25, and its upper edge is 2.3 mm below the current ESP32 copper keepout's y57.7 boundary. This is a planar placement observation, not a manufacturer-approved antenna clearance: the added carrier copper, switch metal and wires still need RF review. Existing traces and vias run beneath the proposed carrier, so it requires dielectric separation and mechanically supported retention; solder mask alone is not an assembly insulator. Do not drill fixing holes through the main board or let switch joints rest on its copper. Rear parts beneath this area also prevent assuming arbitrary through-fastener space.

The nominal front cavity is 10.6 mm above the main PCB. An illustrative stack of **0.8 mm carrier + 0.2 mm solder allowance + 3.5 mm switch = 4.5 mm** leaves **6.1 mm** to the inner front roof before adding insulation, support, adhesive and tolerances. With the carrier underside at z5.6, the free actuator is at z10.1 and a roof-mounted plunger would need approximately 6.45 mm reach to produce the nominal 0.35 mm electrical travel. This is a dimensioning starting point, not a released plunger length or preload. Any added support height raises the switch and reduces that reach. A supported bracket and compliant plunger/hard stop therefore appear geometrically plausible without enlarging the 17.6 mm case, but no carrier layout, solid model or load/tolerance check has been completed. Define which removal must assert tamper: a front-cover plunger can detect cover opening, while detecting removal of the complete reader from the wall requires an actuator coupled to the fixed backplate or mounting surface. Verify that linkage before adopting the front location.

The selected operating force is 2.25 ± 0.55 N; electrical travel is 0.35 mm with +0.25/−0.15 mm tolerance. Electrical travel is not a safe overtravel limit. Use a compliant plunger and a separate hard stop, obtain the permitted sustained load and overtravel limits, and prove closure/release across the full tolerance stack. Leads must not carry the actuation force. See [remote tamper assembly](../docs/tamper-assembly.md) for terminal grouping, the series resistor and acceptance checks.

The tamper carrier/actuator, buzzer acoustic path, gasket, serviceability, drain/vent strategy and optional relay-expansion housing remain unresolved. Lock switching stays outside this enclosure. No physical fit, pull, thermal, RF, ingress, impact, panel or environmental test has been performed.
