# OpenDual Reader A0 architecture

Independent proposed hardware, 26 September 2026. No reference-reader photos, part number, revision, internal measurements or actual wristbands were supplied. This is an editable engineering prototype, not a fabrication release or validated access-control product.

## Electrical partition

Power enters J1 at 9–16 V DC, nominally 12 V. A 0.75 A PTC and series 40 V B340A-13-F Schottky protect the feed; the Littelfuse SMBJ18A shunts transients to ground. Traco TSR 1-2433 and TSR 1-2450 separately produce 3.3 V and 5 V. These are purchased switching modules with internal inductors/control circuitry. Local MLCCs provide bulk and high-frequency bypassing. There is no USB power circuit. J2 requires a 3.3 V logic UART adapter; its 3.3 V pad is a rail sense point, not a power input.

ESP32-WROOM-32E-N4 supplies three UART functions without pin overlap: UART0 programming, UART1 RS-485, UART2 LF receive-only. SPI connects the PN532 module. External pull-downs hold Wiegand, RGB and relay outputs inactive. GPIO14 has a reset pull-up: its driver therefore uses a 2.2 kΩ base pull-down, reviewed separately in component-review.md. EN has a 10 kΩ / 1 µF startup RC network, boot/reset service pads and GPIO0 pull-up. Strapping GPIOs 2/5/12/15 and flash/NC pins are not used as application outputs. Input-only GPIO34 receives LF and GPIO36 reads tamper.

ID Innovations ID-12LA-HE is supplied from 3.3 V; pin 9 feeds UART RX GPIO34 directly. It has an internal LF antenna, matching and proprietary decoder. Pins 3/4 must not connect. It supports a documented subset of HID Prox plus EM4100-compatible tags; this does not establish every HID format or a particular PDK wristband. Its reset is manually accessible but has no MCU pin in A0.

ELECHOUSE PN5321 MINI SKU NFC_EX_MINI hardware V2 uses a 5 V supply and 3.3 V SPI. J3 maps the manufacturer's seven-wire harness; its connector's wire colours and orientation must be checked. Its supplied ferrite antenna and matching are retained, not reverse-engineered. IRQ is not exposed: firmware polls the SPI status asynchronously. It identifies ISO/IEC 14443A UIDs of 4, 7 or 10 bytes. PN532 is not recommended for new designs by NXP; migrating to PN7160 would be a separate hardware/firmware change.

THVD2410DR is a 3.3 V, half-duplex 500 kbps RS-485 device with ±70 V bus fault protection. GPIO4 controls DE and /RE together. A is its noninverting bus pin 6; B is inverting pin 7. SM712 provides external transient clamping. Short cable-entry protection routing and surge testing are required; part ratings are not connector-level certification. JP4 connects 120 Ω termination only at an end of the bus. No local bias resistors are populated; the control panel supplies the single network bias if needed and the transceiver has failsafe reception. Interface is nonisolated and requires a shared signal reference.

Wiegand outputs are NPN open collectors with 2.2 kΩ base resistors, 100 kΩ pull-downs, 47 Ω series resistors and unidirectional SMBJ15A protection. Operating contract: panel pull-up of 0–12 V nominal, a 15 V absolute design limit, and ≤5 mA per line. At 5 mA, 47 Ω drops 0.235 V; conservatively adding 0.2 V saturation gives 0.435 V low. Actual worst-case across temperature remains a bench check. GPIO HIGH asserts the sink; neither line has a pull-up to MCU power. There are no external panel LED/buzzer inputs in A0; these optional functions are omitted.

RGB LED uses three independently limited 5 V NPN branches. The passive Murata piezo receives 4 kHz pulses through Q6 and a 1 kΩ pull-up; it is not a DC active buzzer. The remote tamper assembly uses the gold-contact Y31B13136FPLFG / KSC223G LFG SPST-NO switch, held closed by the assembled enclosure, and a remote 1 kΩ series resistor that limits C13 discharge current. It pulls GPIO36 LOW through J4 when pressed; a released switch or cut wire produces HIGH and tamper. The carrier, bracket and compliant plunger remain mechanical design work; see [tamper assembly](tamper-assembly.md). J5 is 3.3 V logic only to a separate relay demonstration board. Lock current must never flow through the reader PCB.

## RF and mechanical placement

Six-layer, 1.6 mm FR4 carrier, 36 × 124 mm, with a 12 × 10 mm bottom cable notch. Six layers provide space through the narrow corridors beside the RF reserves and a dedicated In3.Cu GND layer. The other inner layers and both outer layers carry routing with GND fills; every RF keepout applies to all six layers. Stackup assumptions are 35 µm outer and 18 µm inner copper. A fabricator-specific dielectric stackup, impedance and laminate are not locked. RF exclusions interrupt even the dedicated ground layer, so return paths and plane necks still require review. Board constraints target 0.20 mm copper clearance, 0.20 mm signal traces, 0.6/0.3 mm vias and 0.30 mm copper-to-edge clearance. Power routing must be assessed for continuous current and return paths. Routing and planes near antennas must remain out of the explicit rule areas. Do not treat the nominal stackup as controlled-impedance certification. The earlier four-layer trials are historical; adding two copper layers does not change the outline or nominal board/enclosure thickness.

The ESP32 PCB antenna is at the upper end, HF ferrite antenna in a separate band below it, LF module below the mounting corridor, HF electronics below LF, and switching modules near the cable exit. The manufacturer ESP32 footprint keepout is retained. Nearby metal screws/mullions, cable bends, ferrite and final plastic can detune all antennas. LF and HF are automatically available without user mode selection, but simultaneous field operation is unmeasured. If coexistence fails, an LF-reset GPIO ECO and bounded interleaved acquisition firmware are necessary; A0 cannot promise this without modification.

## Firmware modes and trust boundary

Exactly one mode is compiled: OSDP_PD (panel authorizes), WIEGAND_READER (panel receives full explicitly mapped values), STANDALONE_DEMO (empty-by-default exact UID whitelist and 3-second external relay). The separately compiled provisioning image is a maintenance tool, not a fourth access mode. LibOSDP 3.2.6 runs in PD mode and enforces Secure Channel with a unique provisioned SCBK; there is no silent plaintext fallback. Keys are absent from source. See firmware/README.md for wiring, provisioning and remaining physical-key-extraction limitations.

No Seos, proprietary HID application, mobile credential or Apple Wallet support is claimed. UID whitelisting is identification for demonstrations, not cryptographic credential authentication. HID raw-frame ambiguities are preserved rather than guessed; 26-bit panel mapping remains disabled until captures establish actual bit order/parity.

The nominal six-layer thickness table and fabrication assumptions are in [stackup.md](stackup.md).
