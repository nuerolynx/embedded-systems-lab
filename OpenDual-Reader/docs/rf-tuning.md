# RF implementation and measurement plan

Status: engineering prototype. No antenna, field strength, read range, coexistence, EMC, or final enclosure measurements have been performed. Values below marked as choices are design choices to test, not measured performance.

## LF module and complete data

The selected ID Innovations ID-12LA-HE has an internal LF antenna. Pins 3 and 4 remain unconnected: they are not an external-coil connector on this model. No guessed LF resonating capacitor is added to the carrier. Manufacturer support covers EM4001-compatible framing and HID H10301/similar 44-user-bit short formats, excluding 84-user-bit long formats. The EM4100 Manchester-64 family is the intended EM compatibility case. Confirm actual tags on the bench. [LF source](https://www.id-innovations.com/ID-3%2612%2620LA-HE%28en%29new.pdf), [EM framing reference](https://www.emmicroelectronic.com/sites/default/files/products/datasheets/em4200_ds.pdf).

The LF module continuously monitors its EM/HID receive schemes. This does not establish coexistence with the separate HF field. The module's internal decoder firmware is not available as source. Its processor-facing serial output is pin 9, 9600 baud, 8-N-1, following datasheet section 12.1. Pin 8 is the pseudo-RS232 connection in section 12. The pin-table word "inverted" is relative to that opposite output; verify idle-high UART and frames on the bench. In this carrier, the LF supply is 3.3 V and pin 9 connects to GPIO34. Never substitute a 5 V LF rail without adding translation. LF reset has a 10 kΩ pull-up and manual shorting provision; it has no assigned firmware GPIO.

Preserve raw LF frames and all EM data bytes. The provisional LF datasheet has conflicting HID character-count/nibble descriptions and an EM checksum description inconsistent with its example. Do not silently reinterpret the HID payload as an access-control number. Capture actual bytes from known credentials, document the observed format, and enable H10301 extraction only after bit ordering and parity are verified. Preserve the original frame beside any explicitly configured Wiegand mapping.

WBC65/WBC75 are documented 125 kHz HID-compatible 26-bit wristbands; another PDK credential model may use different technology. The user has supplied no actual wristband identification. [PDK source](https://support.pdk.io/hc/en-us/articles/34384561871127-Wristband-Credentials-Data-Sheet).

## HF module boundary

Use ELECHOUSE PN5321 MINI **NFC_EX_MINI, hardware V2**, with its supplied 10 × 25 mm ferrite-backed antenna. The board envelope is 25 × 16.4 × 4.4 mm. Supply accepts 3.3–5.0 V; all host signals use 3.3 V logic. The module's seven-pin host connector is: 1 CS, 2 MOSI, 3 MISO, 4 SCK, 5 active-low RSTPD_N, 6 supply, 7 ground. SPI uses mode 0, LSB first, at no more than 5 MHz. Set only the SPI solder bridge with power removed. [Exact module manual](https://www.elechouse.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Elechouse-Pn5321-Mini-Product-Manual-V1.pdf).

The host interface does not expose IRQ. Firmware must poll the SPI ready/status indication without unbounded waiting. The reset connection is a real hardware reset/power-down input, preferably driven open drain. The purchased module incorporates PN532 ROM firmware and its matching network; this carrier project does not recreate or claim an open implementation of those internals. Connect only one antenna connector. Carrier harness numbering must be checked pin-to-pin against the module; connector pitch alone does not establish mating compatibility.

Scope is ISO/IEC 14443A identification, including full available MIFARE Classic/Ultralight UID length. PN532 support does not establish credential authentication or support for Seos, proprietary HID applications, Apple Wallet, mobile credentials, or ISO15693. Preserve UID length explicitly; a long UID must not be silently squeezed into Wiegand-26. PN532 lifecycle status is a production concern: [NXP lists it as not recommended for new designs](https://www.nxp.com/products/rfid-nfc/nfc-hf/nfc-readers/nfc-integrated-solution:PN5321A3HN).

## Placement choices to validate

- Place LF and HF antennas in separate zones along the mullion height, with parallel planes facing the credential. Do not stack their loop areas. Reserve a tunable separation margin before fixing the enclosure clips.
- Initial mechanical coordinates are LF body center (118,116) mm and HF antenna center (118,88) mm in the PCB coordinate system; HF is 25 mm wide by 10 mm high. This yields 10.05 mm edge separation using the maximum LF height 25.9 mm. This spacing is a geometric proposal, not RF validation.
- Keep host-board copper and components out of each antenna projection on **every copper layer**, except the module's documented host pads and unavoidable short escape connections outside the active loop. A ground plane is useful under digital/power circuitry, not under an LF/HF loop.
- Retain the HF antenna's supplied ferrite between antenna and rear metal. Do not substitute an unspecified noise absorber. Ferrite permeability and loss are frequency dependent; HF ferrite suitability does not prove 125 kHz suitability. No added LF ferrite material is released in the BOM until measured with the ID module.
- Put switching power circuitry and high-current loops in the cable/logic region. Keep antenna leads away from switching nodes, the RS-485 cable and buzzer drive loops. Route supply/return together and avoid making a cable loop around either antenna.
- Keep metal fasteners outside loop areas. Include the real mullion, rear cable and all screws in final testing. Nonmetal front materials, coatings, glue, potting and water ingress can change the tune.
- Preserve the ESP32 module's independent antenna keepout and edge placement. LF/HF ferrite, cable bundles and screws must not enter that space. Use the exact [Espressif module placement guidance](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp32-wroom-32e_esp32-wroom-32ue_datasheet_en.html).

The implemented carrier rules are provisional. The HF antenna and ESP32 antenna regions prohibit tracks, vias and pours. The larger LF region prohibits pours, while its central region also prohibits tracks and vias; narrow signal traces can therefore pass around the LF module's perimeter. This is a layout compromise, not evidence that the sealed LF antenna tolerates that copper. Compare it with an isolated module and measure detuning, current and read performance before treating the routing as an RF release. Preserve all four rule polygons and extend their existing restrictions to every copper layer whenever the stack changes.

## Measurement sequence

1. Photograph and measure the purchased modules, antenna, ferrite and leads. Confirm revisions and host pinout before powering. Retain supplier antenna lead length initially; shortening it changes the RF network.
2. Establish each reader's baseline alone on a nonconductive fixture. Use a current-limited supply, capture rail current and ripple, and record a grid of repeatable credential positions and angles. Include the exact PDK wristband, a known EM4100-family tag, MIFARE Classic and Ultralight samples with different UID lengths.
3. Characterize LF with a small, weakly coupled pickup loop and oscilloscope/spectrum analyzer. Measure carrier frequency, envelope, current, startup after reset and errors. Avoid loading the sealed antenna or applying a grounded probe across an RF node. Since this module has no exposed tuning network, adjust placement or obtain supplier tuning support instead of attaching random capacitors.
4. For HF, use the supplier's completed antenna first. If retuning is required, obtain the module matching schematic and target antenna-port impedance. Calibrate a VNA at the actual fixture plane; disconnect and depower the radio before an impedance measurement. Never assume the antenna connector is a generic 50-ohm port. For an isolated coil, measure inductance and resistance at the frequency specified by the guide and sweep through self resonance. Recheck with ferrite and enclosure assembled. [NXP measurement method](https://www.nxp.com/docs/en/application-note/AN11535.pdf).
5. Only after the topology is known, derive matching values from measured complex impedance and NXP's method; check transmitter current and receiver voltage limits. A resonance estimate such as C = 1/((2πf)^2 L) is a calculation aid, not a complete PN532 matching network. Damping, EMC filter and parasitic capacitance remain part of the design. [NXP RF design guide](https://community.nxp.com/pwmxy87654/attachments/pwmxy87654/wireless-connectivity/12935/3/AN1445.pdf).
6. Repeat LF-only, HF-only and simultaneous operation at the final spacing, with ESP32 radio off and active, buzzer on/off, and cable traffic. Record minimum/maximum supply conditions, enclosure and metal backing. Capture false reads, missed reads, raw frames and time from credential presentation to event delivery.
7. If coexistence is inadequate, automatic excitation interleaving would require an ECO adding GPIO control of LF reset, because this carrier provides only manual LF reset. PN532 RF control alone cannot time-slice a continuously transmitting LF module. Do not claim automatic interleaving is already implemented. After the ECO, measure startup/settling times and schedule both credential families without user mode selection. Report worst-case acquisition latency as the sum of the competing window, settling and acquisition durations, then validate panel behavior. No unmeasured timing window is a released value.
8. Finish with environmental, ESD and emissions testing appropriate to the intended installation. RF read testing is not a substitute for those checks.

## Test record to complete

For every result record board/module revision, credential model, full raw ID/UID and length, antenna/ferrite part, material stack, metal clearance, lead arrangement, temperature, supply voltage, LF/HF activation policy, instrument/fixture calibration, measurement file, successful reads/attempts, false events, and latency distribution. No read-range promise is made by this revision.
